# Chapter IX - The Fjord

It's pretty hard to go about one's real business when you've been left out in the cold. Dora was atill fuming inside at the folly; yes the folly; the Houtrakkers and that wispy imp Tako, in particular, had left her in.

The expectant evening, hustling into the Cold War bomb shelter under the Fjord community centre. She had been mortified - she'd brought all her friends; vague acquaintances; groupies; roadies and a rando busker or two. Even Mr. Ate had slipped away unseen when he'd had half a chance. The worst was Molly's look of derision and worst of all, pity. 

The stage had hummed with 50Hz, a single breath was enough to tilt it into banshee-style eardrum-shattering feedback whines. Primed like a grenade, the sound engineer had rustled up all the vacuum tubes, condenser mikes, and guitar footpedals Jimi Hendrix would have been proud of — he’d had to pilfer the juice from the daycare centre just to fuel the 1960s pedal by EHX _Electric Lady_ a vintage beaut.

Too bad the little moppets from the daycare centre would have tepid chestnut milk tomorrow. No doubt enough cause for the authorities to launch an investigation; uncover hacking; blame it on spies in the PTA; close the centre for weeks; have the urchins wander the streets nicking and begging straight out of Dickens.

Benevolent side-mission accomplished.

It was an unspoken truth that the brainwashing they were getting — added to their milk, some said — was no joke. In the stifling straitjacket that was the daycare centre, the harbinger of zombie model citizens, their minds were hobbled from day one. What better than to have them fend for themselves: mucking about in the streets, playing mock trench warfare, getting scabby knees, falling out of trees, burning cats’ tails, and stealing lunch from the old fogeys’ walker-mounted shopping bags — new recruits for the partisaners.

The risks she'd taken, holing up in the broom closet of the daycare centre with Molly... They had prodded Molly's niece to help out - she worked in the daycare center and was deliciously and unabashedly naive. So when Molly said she needed to practise her broom-sweeping for the local contest, she said, brilliant good luck it's all yours, and invited us stay after the closing song celebrating the Exalted Leader had finished, giving us the code of the alarm box as well. The code was what we had already surmised, namely the birthday of our Exalted Leader, Dustin, 22_11_63.

We stole some of the protein bars (_only 55 % insect, 45% labgrown goodness_) that were reserved for the young tykes. God, they were good; if a bit crunchy. Chinese South-West Africa had experienced a particularly fecund locust bumper crop last spring.

The stolen kisses in the closet — committed to memory forever because of the cloying taste of the sticky protein bar and the whiff of lemony smell from the detergents stored there — were the best pastime ever, waiting for the curfew and the dark. A lot better than playing sudoku, as she might have done in a more solitary age, thought Dora exultantly.

She recalled the excitement of stealing the protein bars and mushroom milk from the daycare centre, and displaying them as prizes in the Fjord Bar. Everyone had found means to evade the curfew and the drones hovering on rooftops.
Some had perched fake gulls on wavy long sticks or bamboo with a spring on their heads, setting the drones into evasive mode and not into patrolling mode. Others had printed bricks on their clothes and hoods so as to blend into the background. They had thin aluminium foil woven into their clothes so as not to emit any infrared waves.

There was a peak interest in sewing and weaving machines, and Dora knew that Puck, for one, had programmed an old industrial knitting machine from the eighties — taking orders and trading patterns on the Dusky Web, and in his job as a bathroom salesman.

Buzzing with the glucose high from the protein bars, stealing a box or two of, excitement of, and chestnut milk, displaying them as proud conquests in the Fjord bar. “Hey Mr. Ate, glad you made it!” she greeted her old pal with a complicated-looking but totally random handshake sequence, which was their little thing since uni. Mr. Ate’s hand was sweatier than usual, and he was scanning the modest crowd packed into the cellar.

Embracing her briefly but tightly nonetheless, he greeted her back, saying, “Yo, airy girl, let me cruise to the bar see how it's going there!"

“Do check out the protein bars and chestnut milk, straight from the tyke-place, courtesy of Molly and her niece, how about that,” Dora quipped.

“Yo really, is Molly even here?” replied Mr. Ate with unusual surprise. “I thought she didn’t dig these ska/reggae type fissas...”

“Okay well it’s about to start, go get yourself a protein bar before they run out!” 

The roadies were done fixing the stage, the lighting, the cables and the mikes, drums and as usual took way longer than if Dora had done it herself, and the scene was now ripe for a giant legendary party. The song came to an end, and the last roadie jumped down from the stage and an expectant hum like a current went through the crowd.

Mr. Ate whispered something to the sound engineer, who nodded, and soon the song _There’s a Rat in the Kitchen Tonight_ by the nineteen eighties band UB-40 revved up and the crowd exploded in mass hysteria, singing along at the top of their lungs.

~ *** ~

The ageing smoke machine belched erratic clouds of whitish muck over the expectant and fidgety crowd. Dora motioned to Molly to hustle to the front of the stage, as she saw the sound engineer twiddle the last knobs on the guitar amp, and he bustled off-stage, a sure sign things were about to blast off. The famous Houtrakkers, or as their stage name was, 2BoN2B, and she was dying to see them outside of their hidey-hole. Were they even of flesh and blood? Hard to tell...

Mr. Ate took to the stage as the crowd yelled and catcalled. "Now announcing the moment you've all been waiting for... I proudly present the best punk rock band since Sonic Youth, 2BON2B!!!" 

The stage was already hard to make out because of the smoke, now the lights went full on red, and the smoke machine full blast - too much for its antiquated circuitry, it roared, belched, and sparked and misted like Krakatoa and the ensuing short-circuit blew the lights - the crowd was in dismay and it was utter pitch black - Dora could only hear a swish and imagined a residual retina imprint of Mr. Ate shooing off pronto to the left of the stage. 

"Where's he going? Molly, where is the band - Molly?" She grasped in the blind to her right, where Molly had been. There was no Molly in her faux silk blouse beside her, only what felt like tatty woolly sweater. 

A gravely voice said, "Yo I'm no Molly, damn it's dark," and the owner of the voice lit a lighter, as did others in the crowd. At the bar in the back a flashlight was on and someone was rummaging for matches to promptly light some emergency candles. Dora sped round on her axis, neither Molly nor Mr. Ate were anywhere to be seen. 

"WTF," exclaimed Dora, and grabbed for her bike light deep in her jeans pocket. "Where are they? The john, most likely," and she ran onto the stage, tripping over the spaghetti of cables and falling over the crouching sound engineer who lamented the demise of the mist machine. She tumbled down the left of the stage, through the curtains and suddenly remembered the Cold War bomb shelter tunnel access. 

_That must be where they went, though WTF, WTF, WTF,_ she thought, and felt around in the dark until she came to the cold steel door, and pushed down hard on the blast door handle - and it swung open to a dark gray dimness. Dora suddenly grew very cold, gripped by fear, like a tight cold hand constricting her throat and chest. _What if Molly ran off with Mr. Ate,_ she despaired, and stumbled on into the tunnel lit by intermittent old sodium lamps flickering dimly so she could see her way around. She moved into the hallway stretching back quite far into the gloom. She couldn't remember anything about how far the tunnels reached nor where they went. 

Pausing in the gloom at a crossroads, catching her breath and feeling wretched at the double betrayal, first the Houtrakkers not showing, secondly her pal and girlfriend eloping, she heard footsteps approaching behind her. She had to hide, fast. In the passageway to her right, she saw a door ajar to darkness. She ran in and hid behind the door, just able to see part of the corridor. She saw a pair of legs and booted feet sneak past. It wasn't Mr. Ate, nor Molly - it was the hairy punk who had stood right next to her. _What a coincidence, she thought. Or was it?_ 

He carried a powerful flashlight and its narrow beam was almost blinding. He appeared to look for footsteps in the dust. _How did he get that military grade flashlight? OMG hope he won't find me,_ thought Dora, holding her breath. But on he went, past Dora's hiding place in a Cold War broom closet. 

Almost out of sight, he suddenly looked up, as if he heard a noise further down the corridor and he turned and snuck thataway quickly. _What the heck is this all about?_ and the thought of something fishy made Dora get her resolve and on the spur of the moment she slid out to follow him, albeit at a safe distance, staying a corner behind so as to avoid being spotted if he happened to look behind him. 

She was only a few minutes into her prowling and the punk was around another corner, when she heard a loud crunch! and an oomph! and a thud. She glanced furtively around the corner and saw what looked like Mr. Ate dragging the punk into a doorway and closing it. _WTF Mr. Ate got him! He's going to be neck-deep in shit! WTF!_ 

Dora decided this was too much for her and she darted back through the corridors back, now hearing faint drumming, to the stagedoor behind the curtains. Carefully she pushed down on the lever to open the blast door, greeted by fervent drumming, and moved forward to peer through the curtains, the lights had come on again and and the spaghetti removed. Two yembe players pelted out a whack rhythm, and Molly was spinning around playing a mad tune on a kazoo and Puck had found a small flute playing and prancing around like a satyr. The crowd seemed to have forgotten about having been stood up, and was merry and well sloshed.  

Molly spotted Dora and jumped towards her and pulled her forward onto the stage and kissed her, "There you are! Where've you been! You're missing out! Come on let's go and have a pogo," and grabbed her hand and jumped off the stage into the roaring, foaming, tanked up crowd.
