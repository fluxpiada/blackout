# Chapter IX - The Fjord

It's pretty hard to go about one's real business when you've been left out in the cold. Dora was atill fuming inside at the folly; yes the folly; the Houtrakkers and that wispy imp Tako, in particular, had left her in.

The expectant evening, hustling into the Cold War bomb shelter under the Fjord community centre.

She had been mortified - she'd brought all her friends; vague acquaintances; groupies; roadies and a rando busker or two. Even Bongwe had slipped away unseen when he'd had half a chance. The worst was Molly's look of derision and worst of all, pity. 

The stage had hummed with 50Hz, a single breath was enough to tilt it into banshee-style eardrum-shattering feedback whines. Primed like a grenade, the sound engineer had rustled up all the vacuum tubes, condenser mikes, and guitar footpedals Jimi Hendrix would have been proud of — he’d had to pilfer the juice from the daycare centre just to fuel the 1960s pedal by EHX _Electric Lady_ a vintage beaut.

Too bad the little moppets from the daycare centre would have tepid chestnut milk tomorrow. No doubt enough cause for the authorities to launch an investigation; uncover hacking; blame it on spies in the PTA; close the centre for weeks; have the urchins wander the streets nicking and begging straight out of Dickens.

Benevolent side-mission accomplished.

Because the brainwashing they were getting — added to their milk, some said — was no joke. In the stifling straitjacket that was the daycare centre, the harbinger of zombie model citizens, their minds were hobbled from day one. What better than to have them fend for themselves: mucking about in the streets, playing mock trench warfare, getting scabby knees, falling out of trees, burning cats’ tails, and stealing lunch from the old fogeys’ walker-mounted shopping bags — new recruits for the partisaners.

The risks she'd taken, holing up in the broom closet of the daycare centre with Molly... They had prodded Molly's niece to help out - she worked in the daycare center and was deliciously and unabashedly naive. So when Molly said she needed to practise her broom-sweeping for the local contest, she said, brilliant good luck it's all yours, and invited us stay after the closing song celebrating the Exalted Leader had finished, giving us the code of the alerm box as well. The code was what we had already surmised, namely the birthday of our Exalted Leader, Dustin, 22_11_63.

We stole some of the protein bars (_only 55 % insect, 45% labgrown goodness_) that were reserved for the young tykes. God, they were good; if a bit crunchy. Chinese South-West Africa had experienced a particularly fecund locust bumper crop last spring.

The stolen kisses in the closet — committed to memory forever because of the cloying taste of the sticky protein bar and the whiff of lemony smell from the detergents stored there — were the best pastime ever, waiting for the curfew and the dark. A lot better than playing sudoku, as she might have done in a more solitary age, thought Dora exultantly.

Buzzing with the glucose high from the protein bars, stealing a box or two of, excitement of, and chestnut milk, displaying them as proud conquests in the Fjord bar. Everyone had found means to evade the curfew and the drones hovering on rooftops — some had perched fake gulls on wavy long sticks with a spring on their heads, setting the drones into evasive mode instead of patrol mode.

Others had printed bricks on their clothes and hoods so as to blend into the background — and had thin aluminium foil woven into their clothes so as not to emit any infrared waves. There was a peak interest in sewing and weaving machines; and Dora knew that Puck, for one, had reprogrammed an old industrial knitting machine from the eighties, taking orders and trading patterns on the Dusky Web — and in his job as a bathroom salesman.
