
# Zoning for Success

On her way to work, she was still fuming. She felt the Houtrakkers had let her down by not coming to the Fjord and she had no way of contacting the Houtrakkers or get the telephone sets to them. She’d have to enlist Erwin’s help to get back to the bicycle; for which she could think of no logical explanation unless she involved him in this web of spionage. And he was too sweet to be involved in that. 

Neither did she look forward to hoisting herself into that godawful green suit-stock­ing again. Speaking of which — where had she left that darn thing? The blood drained from her face when she realised she might have lost it, fallen out of her pocket. She had no idea in the slightest when last she had felt that creepy, slick stocking in her parka pocket.

The train rocketed out of the Halfweg tunnel, and she involuntarily braced herself for the impact. The abrasions and bruises were only just fading. She took a deep breath, and surreptitiously scanned her fellow commuters’ countenances — were they really all that amnesiac? How entirely effective the combination of propaganda, emotional dampening, structural attention-draining, retro-wiping, and mild malnutrition were to keep the man down. Where was the stocking?!

Barely a week ago, the train had been filled with thousands of other people — these gits were their replacements. They  were as sweet as they come, as meek as lambs. Casually scrolling the news bulletins and regurgitated torpid drivel, as if they weren’t literally building on the bones, riding over the cremated accordion of thousands of lives. Surely, some of them must have had relatives. Surely, some must feel a deep sense of loss, surely some must feel guilty taking their place.

Yes, there had been a period of mourning. With the invitation to every funeral came a small package of government-prescribed _Painquell_ — five capsules that would knock your socks off and fry your memory. You’d only wake for a glass of water and a capsule once every twenty-four hours.

Of course! That’s why it had been so quiet in the streets. That’s why they had been able to evade, duck, dive, and hide so effectively. The better half of Schoten had been flattened in a drug-induced stupor.

And that’s why everyone in the train was more doped up than usual.

Dora exited the SubT one stop earlier, Spierinxhoorn, where, just opposite the soccer fields, she was greeted with an iron gate picturing Our Dear Leader, Dustin, beckoning the proles from the past to step into the glorious future of the Free Economic Era. She wanted to walk the last two clicks and take the time to anticipate on how her comrades might see her with suspicion and anticipate any insiduous remarks with a glib retort. 

Underlying that resolve, was her fervent hope that the Houtrakkers could be relied on to have informed her workplace and chef Phil that her absence was indeed credible and justified. Were they to be trusted? After the no-show at the Fjord, she was deeply unsure. 

And her chef, Phil Cortana, was a conniving, wormtongue mouthbreather of a creature, and not easily duped. He had been on her case since last year at the XDay party – hunching too close to her, his vile breath from a long-neglected halitosis curdling the very air. His eyes bloodshot from the nitrogen dioxide emissions, his voice hoarse from the fracking chemicals leaching into the water supply, he rasped, "My department is short on Neg Points, ms. Olivetti, so just beware, I might need to push you out of the Zone of Success and into Reticulating Splines!" 

Dora shuddered at the memory, and the blustery wind grew in strength and blew wetly from the West through her parka.

Reticulating Splines was a re-integration, or demotion, where one was assigned to the twenty-second floor, the Testing Floor. It was one step removed from being sent-off permanently - the fate of many too overtly intelligentia - to the open-pit lithium mines in Chinese Congo. It was dangerous to appear a know-it-all, and Dora, Erwin and Tricia dumbed down their speech and often removed their glasses in public when they could.

She recalled her first day at the department, not so long ago. 

"Welcome, Ms. Olivetti, esteemed comrade, to our humble department!" said Mr. Phil Cortana, her new Supervisor — or as he was named, Shift Leader level 1.01. He waved her into the hallway of the 11th Floor. The lights were rather dim, since the solar power hadn’t the force at seven o'clock in the morning to power them properly. It was still dark outside and it was a glum sight. The Westelijk Havengebied was populated with morose and rickety ships, cranes dilapidated, forking crummy metal waste into big bins meant for resmelting, in the forges that were belching brown clouds of lignite exhaust. In the gloom behind the exhaust pipes loomed a squat black building blotting out half of the northern horizon. 

Mr. Cortana wiggled his eyebrows as he gestured for her to pass her coat to hang on the sparse and dripping coatrack in the corner of the hallway. "Pass me your coat please, ms.,” he said, and she quickly though clumsily took off her coat and grotty scarf.

"What a nice view," she crooned, passing him her weathered parka, "I always wondered how far one could see from this impressive building! Is that the New Economic Data Box - I mean Data Archive?”

"Oh yes, well this isn’t the half of it, just wait until you see old Schoten when it gets lighter!" He puffed with pride, as if he had personally designed the view himself. He held up her bundled coat and scarf as if it was a wet cat, far away from him, and hung it on an unoccupied hook on the coat rack. 

Mr. Phil Cortana was dressed that day - and every day as she would learn - in khaki slacks, ironed to perfection, and a white dress shirt that was old and a bit tatty around the wrists, but again, fastidiously creased. He shook his left hand ostentatiously so that a gleaming wristwatch emerged from under his sleeve. 

Obviously she couldn’t give a rat’s arse about her supervisor's outdated taste in fashion accessories, but understood that it would flatter her new boss’s ego, and therefore was an invitation she couldn’t nor shouldn’t refuse. Better yet, it would probably go quite a way towards getting her first Pos Point.

"Wow," exclaimed Dora with Oscar-material faked surprise, "Is that a 20th century Seiko or Casio? I’m impressed — it’s quite the ordeal to keep them old LCDs going!"

"Thank you, that’s quite right, it’s a nineteen nineties Seiko, and I found some old batteries in my late father’s inheritance so I got it to work again, like clockwork, hi hi hi," he tittered, chuffed at his little double-entendre.  

Phil shook his left wrist once again in that irritating manner befitting hirsute middle-aged men of Mediterranean descent. Dora decided to compliment him on his restoration skills but thought he was a doofus because he didn’t know how to adjust the metal clasps of his wristwatch to fit his wrist better.

 "I suppose taking good care of old materials is a merit for this job, isn’t it?"
 
"Absolutely," blustered Phil, his unibrow wiggling like a fat black caterpillar, "and your powers of observation and knowledge of antiques is duly noted! Follow me, I’ll show you your work counter, or charrel as we call it, and introduce you to your two closest scribe comrades."

He shuffled forward out of the antechamber followed by Dora, the latest addition to the legion of cognitarian drudges and entered the work floor -  a giant expanse of low small desks and monk-like scribes poring over low-lit computer screens and tapping away at high speeds. It sounded like the trillions of wings of a vast cloud of locusts — a constant droning that was rather calming in a rather numbing fashion. 

Her guide’s shuffling gait sped up as he nimbly slalomed from left to right through the labyrinthine office. Somewhere left of the middle of the complex Phil ended his ballet through the office space with a little flourish of the hips. 

 "Well pay attention comrades Erwin and Tricia, here she is, your new comrade, Ms. Dora Olivetti."
 
The two drudges looked up, both bespectacled, simultaneously. Out of habit as not to appear like intelligentsia, they also took off their glasses simultaneously. Their shining smiles, dilated pupils and blushing cheeks showed that they had had ample experience with Voigt-Kampff tests.

The man, a bit pudgy and squint in his left eye, was a stooping hulk of a man, but had a friendly face and a weak, wet handshake. To compensate for that emasculating trait, he pumped her hand up and down like a piston.

 "Erwin, Erwin Adler is the name, nice to meet you, comrade Dora,” he said, his voice soft, and a bit monotonous. Despite the squint in his eye, he made quite the effort to pin her gaze down, and his handshake was becoming sweaty and uncomfortable.

Phil Cortana, who must once have aspired to greatness like the naive rest of us; aspire to becoming a Blade Runner perhaps - before his brows met each other above the bridge of his Roman nose. Yet when his Pokémon skills faltered at level forty-nine, when his Fortnite skills were staying delulu, round about that time he must have realised the limitations to his character and career advancement were as stark as the unibrow harshly delineating his visage. 

A bonus of his bristly bricky was he needed no Voigt-Kampff test — he was the master of the monobrow, and it indicated his moods as accurately as a barometer the air pressure. Dora felt she could have done worse than Tricia and Erwin, they seemed genuinely welcoming. 

“Well, thanks guys, look forward to learning from you. And, of course also especially from you, Shift Leader Cortana!"

“Tricia, pay extra attention to retro-wiping those passages on alternative green energy sources like ion drives, subspace plotters, hyperdrives, and solar parasols, there are new quarterly KPIs attached to these!” and he waved a long skinny finger to emphasise the gravitas.
