
# Zoning For Success

On her way to work, she was still fuming. She had no way of contacting the Houtrak­kers; she’d have to enlist Erwin’s help again — and think of a way to pluck the look of the bike. 
<!--Or does she? -->
And hoist herself into that godawful green stocking again. Speaking of which, where had she left that darn thing? The blood drained from her face when she realised she might have lost it — fallen out of her pocket? She had no idea when she had last felt that creepy slick stocking in her parka pocket. 

The train rocketed out of the Halfweg tunnel, and she involuntarily braced herself for the impact. The abrasions and bruises were only just fading. 

She surreptitiously scanned her fellow commuters’ countenances — were they really all that amnesiac? How entirely effective the combination of propaganda, emotional dampening, structural attention-draining, retro-wiping, and mild malnutrition were — to keep the man down. 

Barely a week ago, the train had been filled with thousands of other people. These gits were their replacements! And they were as sweet as they come — as meek as lambs. Casually scrolling the news bulletins and the regurgitated doom-scrolling drivel, as if they weren’t literally building on the bones, riding over the cremated accordion of thousands of lives. 

Surely, some of them must have had relatives. Surely some must feel a deep sense of loss — and guilt at taking their place.

Yes, there had been a period of mourning. With the invitation to every funeral came a small package of government-prescribed _Painquell_ — five capsules that would knock your socks off and fry your memory.

You’d only wake for a glass of water and a capsule once every 24 hours. 

Of course! That’s why it had been so quiet in the streets! That’s why they had been able to evade, duck, dive, and hide so effectively. The better half of Schoten had been flattened in a drug-induced fugue, squalor, hibernation. And that’s why everyone in the train was more doped up than usual.
