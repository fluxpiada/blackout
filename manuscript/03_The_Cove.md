
# Chapter III - The Cove

The machines.

Dora had never been a Luddite; quite the contrary. She embraced AI the second it became free of charge (never mind the incessant product placement). Vlogging 10-20 hours a week (a clunky satire on Tamagotchis) and harvesting swathes of followers and a reputation - yet her enjoyment was ephemeral. Something was off, and it wasn't her kefir. 

Only twelve months earlier, happy chappy chirpiness had been exuded by all those who contracted AI: whether their bank accounts were glutted with too many zeroes or just poor punters. It was the witless rubbernecking that bothered her. It made her old elbow fracture ache.

Brace yourself, winter is coming.


Sure, AI was mind-bogglingly fast, sure, it was convenient, sure, the wife bots were a hit with the incels and good riddance to them all, sure, it cut through the vapid bloatware the internet had become. Yet...

## ~ * ~

Fresh out of her literature M.A. she had resigned herself to the expected and bleak cognitariat assignment of training LLMs (dubbed "AI baby rooms") in literature and poetry in basements of disused university libraries. Who was to have predicted that her infatuation with spoofing Tamagotchis would lead to a job interview with the uber-stuffy Ministry of Taxes & Records?

The job interview she had been summoned to attend, was for a semi-permanent work order in the Department of Audits, Double and Crosschecks & Files (the dept. of A, D, C & F for short). The bubbly HR ensign, Ms. Dolores Lasterar, evidently had no idea what the job entailed and could barely enunciate the tasks: "Improving infallibility of records... De-mystifying historical events... Objectifying sentimental causes... Reticulating splines..."

Dora's wild enthusiasm at this one-in-a-million opportunity was abruptly doused by the condition that she stop her vlogging instantly, unequivocally conveyed by ms. Lasterar with flashing eyes. Doable, her followers had been lemminged away to the next fad (AI-generated spaghetti-eating frogs). That potential conflict doused, the interview continued amicably from then on, the ladies sipping their tepid teas from seaweed cups. 

On the subject of Ms. Olivetti's fit for the role, Ms. Lasterar curiously labelled Dora's childlike exuberant vlogging as "manifest... uh, conscientiousness and diligence essential for the apt re-recording of historical documents," reading from the prepped dossier.

Some instinct refrained Dora from correcting Ms. Lasterar’s misconception — not only because the likeable brunette shuffled in her seat and was rather red in the face, what with all the fancy words she had to read, and any affront might cause her to break down in tears— but because being presumed meticulous seemed a key requisite for getting this bonanza of a job. Though it was hard to tell what the job really entailed because of Ms. Lasterar's botched rendition.

"Fiddlesticks, thought Dora, "consider the perks of ample grog and B2-grade lunch thrown in, a no-brainer, yo!" and Dora Olivetti signed her bureaucratic DocuCommit then and there.

Yet her old elbow fracture had throbbed all throughout the interview.

##  ~ * ~

The copse was slightly elevated due to its erstwhile purpose as an Iron Age fortification. Built on the upturned hulls of expended riverine craft; discarded animal bones, shards of pottery, and other flotsam and jetsam. Early forts were essentially rafts, or, for the optimistic, houses on boats. And this one was sheltering an information-age trove.

When Dora was about done in plodding through the sludge, she stubbed her toe on one of these bones— and set off the silent alarm. The copse was overgrown with brambles, hawthorns, and stunted willows. A small gloomy opening in the briars looked like a path to something dry to her, and she crouched down and crawled in. Then strong, hard hands pulled her down, and she tumbled down a slippery slide into a dimly lit cove. Of sorts.

## ~ * ~

“Who the heck are you and how did you survive THAT,” barked a gruff but young voice attached to a skinny young man with a scraggy beard and blonde wispy hair covering what looked like bright blue eyes. On saying “that,” he waved his gangly arm in a broad arc like a crooked windmill. After sliding down, Dora was sat on her butt in a greenish dome that was rather a nasty, dirty, wet hole, filled with the ends of worms and an oozy smell.

“Look, dude, I just got here and got lucky, so give me a break!” she snapped. “And for that matter, who the heck are you and what are YOU doing here?!”

Only then did she notice three humanoid creatures crouching a few metres back, in the gloam of what looked like a corridor. Scared these might be partisans or rebels or incels; or the unvaccinated; the unpassed - in any case probably hungry and violent - she made an appeasing gesture with her hands.

“Look, it was a mess, I don’t know what happened. I knocked my head. But the lights went wild in the SubT and then we got like a power surge and then the whole thing shot out of the Halfwegtunnel and derailed and everyone was dead or dying on impact except me,” she explained hurriedly. “So I know as much as you do, okay. Oh yeah, my VisaPass I tossed into the fire, so I'm nobody now. By the way, my name’s Dora, and you…?" she extended her right hand.

The dude looked relieved, smiled a skewed smile and extended his right hand and said, "My name is Tako, welcome to the Houtrakkers, our partisan Houtrakkers Cove, Dora."
